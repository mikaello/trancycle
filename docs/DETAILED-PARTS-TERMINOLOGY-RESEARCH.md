# Detailed bicycle-parts terminology

## Recommendation

Add 46 provisional English–Bokmål concepts covering bottom-bracket internals, hub internals, threadless-headset parts, and hydraulic-disc-brake parts.
The proposed set expands the glossary from assembly names to parts a rider or mechanic can actually encounter during inspection, replacement, or service.
It deliberately leaves every entry provisional because manufacturer terminology is evidence of use, not proof of a universal Norwegian standard.

Parallel English and Norwegian manufacturer manuals provide the strongest evidence in this review.
Shimano publishes matched diagrams that directly pair most bottom-bracket, hub, and brake terms, while Trek publishes a matched headset exploded view.[^2][^3][^5][^6][^9][^10][^15][^16]
English repair references from Park Tool and Cane Creek clarify the boundaries between parts that are easily conflated.[^1][^11]

## Selection method

A candidate was included when it met all of these conditions:

- It identifies a physical part rather than only a procedure, measurement, or product standard.
- It is useful outside one model-specific exploded view.
- Its English meaning can be separated from adjacent concepts in a short original definition.
- The proposed Bokmål term has direct bilingual manufacturer support, established Norwegian cycling use, or transparent technical composition.
- Any important construction dependency can be explained without implying that every bicycle contains the part.

Generic washers, O-rings, ordinary fasteners, tools, temporary bleed blocks, and model-specific service units were not added unless their cycling use has a distinct established name.
The result is detailed enough to expose the missing vocabulary without turning every exploded-view line item into a separate glossary concept.

## Proposed concepts

### Bottom bracket

Park Tool distinguishes the bottom bracket as a bearing system, the frame's bottom bracket shell, cups or adapters that hold bearings, and the spindle that joins the cranks.[^1]
Shimano's parallel manuals label the same press-fit illustration with `bottom bracket cups`, `inner cover`, and `spacer` in English, and `adapter`, `innerhylse` or `innerdeksel`, and `avstandsstykke` in Norwegian.[^2][^3]

| English preferred term     | Bokmål preferred term         | Important alternatives                | Decision                                                                                                      |
| -------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| bottom bracket bearing     | lager til kranklager          | —                                     | Use a descriptive Norwegian form because `kranklager` commonly names both the whole assembly and its bearing. |
| bottom bracket cup         | lagerkopp til kranklager      | `kranklagerkopp`; `kranklageradapter` | Prefer the unambiguous part name, but retain Shimano's Norwegian `adapter` as admitted usage.                 |
| bottom bracket inner cover | innerhylse til kranklager     | `innerdeksel til kranklager`          | Retain both Norwegian forms found across Shimano manuals.                                                     |
| bottom bracket spacer      | avstandsstykke til kranklager | `kranklageravstandsstykke`            | Qualify the generic part name so search results retain its context.                                           |
| crank spindle              | krankaksel                    | `bottom bracket spindle`              | Prefer the compound used in Shimano documentation and distinguish it from the generic axle concept.[^4]       |

Bottom-bracket designs differ substantially.
Some bearings press directly into the frame, some sit in cups, some spindles belong to the crank, and some cartridge units combine spindle and bearings.[^1]
Definitions therefore describe function and explicitly avoid presenting one exploded arrangement as universal.

### Hub and freehub

Shimano's paired wheel manuals directly support `hub body`/`navhus`, `hub axle`/`navaksel`, `hub flange`/`navflens`, `cone`/`konus`, `cone cap`/`konusendestykke`, `lock nut`/`låsemutter`, `dust cover`/`støvdeksel`, `seal ring`/`tetningsring`, `spacer`/`avstandsstykke`, `right cap`/`høyre endestykke`, `snap ring`/`låsering`, and `hub ratchet`/`navskralle`.[^5][^6]
The same manuals show loose bearing balls and distinguish a ring-ratchet mechanism from other freehub arrangements.[^5][^6]
SRAM service documentation supplies the missing pawl-type construction, while Norwegian dictionaries support `sperrehake` for a spring-loaded part that prevents reverse motion.[^7][^8]

| English preferred term | Bokmål preferred term  | Important alternatives                | Decision                                                                        |
| ---------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| hub body               | navhus                 | `hub shell`                           | Use Shimano's direct bilingual pair.                                            |
| hub axle               | navaksel               | —                                     | Keep separate from a removable thru axle.                                       |
| hub flange             | navflens               | —                                     | Identify the spoke-carrying flange rather than the complete hub body.           |
| hub cone               | navkonus               | `bearing cone`; `konus`               | Qualify the short manual label outside an exploded view.                        |
| hub cone cap           | konusendestykke        | `cone cap`                            | Keep separate from the cone and the more general hub end cap.                   |
| hub locknut            | låsemutter til nav     | `lock nut`; `låsemutter`              | Qualify a generic fastener that has a specific bearing-adjustment role.         |
| hub dust cover         | støvdeksel til nav     | `dust cover`; `støvhette til nav`     | Retain both Norwegian manufacturer forms.                                       |
| hub seal ring          | tetningsring til nav   | `seal ring`; `tetningsring`           | Keep separate from the rigid dust cover.                                        |
| hub spacer             | avstandsstykke til nav | `spacer`; `avstandsstykke`            | Qualify the generic term by assembly.                                           |
| hub end cap            | endestykke til nav     | `end cap`; `navendestykke`            | Generalize the left/right model-specific label without losing context.          |
| hub bearing ball       | lagerkule til nav      | `bearing ball`; `lagerkule`           | Make the bearing role explicit instead of using only `ball` or `kule`.          |
| hub snap ring          | låsering til nav       | `snap ring`; `låsering`               | Define the split ring seated in a groove.                                       |
| hub ratchet            | navskralle             | `ratchet ring`; `skrallehjul til nav` | Reserve this concept for the toothed ring in a ring-ratchet mechanism.          |
| freehub pawl           | sperrehake i boss      | `pawl`; `sperrehake`; `pal`           | Keep it distinct from a ring ratchet and retain the concise technical synonyms. |
| pawl spring            | fjær til sperrehake    | `leaf spring`; `sperrehakefjær`       | Prefer a transparent phrase while the compact compound awaits editorial review. |

This proposal does not restructure the existing `freehub`, `cassette body`, and `boss` entries.
Those entries already overlap and need a separate concept-boundary review before any preferred label is promoted.

### Threadless headset

Trek's matched service manuals pair `upper bearing cover`, `compression ring`, `upper bearing`, `lower bearing`, and `crown race` with `øvre lagerdeksel`, `kompresjonsring`, `øvre kulelager`, `nedre kulelager`, and `gaffelkonus` in the same numbered drawing.[^9][^10]
Cane Creek's technical guide distinguishes the top cap from the upper bearing cover, explains the centering role of the compression ring, distinguishes a star nut from a compression plug, and describes cups as construction-dependent.[^11]
Norwegian cycling references support `lagerkopp`, integrated and semi-integrated headset distinctions, and `kompresjonsplugg`.[^12][^13]

| English preferred term   | Bokmål preferred term           | Important alternatives                                    | Decision                                                                                              |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| headset cup              | lagerkopp til styrelager        | `styrelagerkopp`                                          | Note that fully integrated headsets have molded bearing seats instead of separate cups.               |
| upper headset bearing    | øvre lager i styrelager         | `upper bearing`; `øvre kulelager`                         | Prefer a context-complete phrase and retain Trek's short drawing label.                               |
| lower headset bearing    | nedre lager i styrelager        | `lower bearing`; `nedre kulelager`                        | Use the same pattern as the upper bearing.                                                            |
| headset bearing cover    | lagerdeksel til styrelager      | `upper bearing cover`; `top cover`; `øvre lagerdeksel`    | Explicitly distinguish this cover from the preload top cap.                                           |
| headset compression ring | kompresjonsring til styrelager  | `compression ring`; `kompresjonsring`                     | Qualify the assembly because Norwegian brake documentation uses the same short term for a hose olive. |
| headset preload bolt     | forspenningsbolt til styrelager | `preload bolt`; `top cap bolt`; `topplokkbolt`            | Name the bolt by its adjustment function, not by a false fastening role.                              |
| compression plug         | kompresjonsplugg                | `expansion plug`; `ekspansjonsplugg`; `kompresjonsmutter` | Keep separate from the star nut normally used in a metal steerer tube.                                |
| fork crown               | gaffelkrone                     | —                                                         | Add the fork structure on which the crown race sits.[^14]                                             |

The existing `top cap`, `star nut`, `crown race`, `steerer tube`, `stem`, and `spacer` concepts remain valid adjacent entries.
The new definitions make their boundaries explicit instead of duplicating them.

These are the parts most likely to be named during routine pad replacement, hose shortening, bleeding, caliper mounting, and rotor installation.

## Ambiguities that need editorial attention

### Assembly and part can share a Norwegian label

`Kranklager` commonly refers to the complete bottom-bracket bearing system and may also be used for an individual replacement bearing.
The proposed `lager til kranklager` is intentionally descriptive so the two concepts do not collapse in search and relations.

### Manufacturer translations can be accurate but too broad

Shimano pairs English `bottom bracket cup` with Norwegian `adapter`.[^2][^3]
Because `kranklageradapter` also names reducers that change spindle or shell standards, `lagerkopp til kranklager` is the proposed preferred label and the manufacturer's term is admitted.

### One Norwegian term can name unrelated shapes

Shimano uses `kompresjonsring` for the deformable olive on a brake hose, while Trek uses `kompresjonsring` for the split centering ring in a headset.[^10][^18]
The proposal qualifies the headset term and prefers `klemring til bremseslange` for the hose part.
Both source forms remain searchable.

### Short exploded-view labels need context

Labels such as `cone`, `cap`, `spacer`, `piston`, `membrane`, and `lock ring` are clear beside a numbered drawing but ambiguous in a general glossary.
Preferred terms therefore add the assembly name, while the short labels remain admitted alternatives.

### Construction variants must not become false part lists

A press-fit bottom bracket may have cups, an integrated headset may not, a cup-and-cone hub has cones and loose balls, and a cartridge-bearing hub does not expose the same parts.
A freehub may use pawls or toothed ratchet rings, and a rotor may use bolts or a center lockring.
The definitions and notes state these constraints instead of implying that all listed parts coexist.

## Deliberate exclusions

Threaded-headset locknuts and adjusting races were not added because the first diagram and strongest Norwegian sources cover modern threadless systems.
Internal-gear-hub planets, clutches, brake units, and shift mechanisms were not added because they form a separate terminology domain and vary significantly by model.
Rim-brake arms, noodles, straddle cables, shoes, pad holders, and return springs were not added because the current visual brake context is a hydraulic disc brake.
Coaster brakes, roller brakes, drum brakes, electronic controls, and proprietary sensor parts likewise need focused research rather than being mixed into this set.

These omissions are suitable follow-up batches.
They should use the same rule: paired bilingual technical drawings where available, an original boundary definition, and provisional status until expert review.

## Editorial recommendation

Merge the 46 concepts as provisional suggestions with their sources, definitions, notes, and relations.
Before promoting any entry to reviewed, ask at least one Norwegian bicycle mechanic and one terminology editor to check naturalness, concept boundaries, and the preferred/admitted ranking.
The highest-priority review items are `lager til kranklager`, `lagerkopp til kranklager`, `øvre/nedre lager i styrelager`, `klemring til bremseslange`, `sperrehake i boss`, and `fjær til sperrehake` because these intentionally favor clarity over the shortest workshop label.
